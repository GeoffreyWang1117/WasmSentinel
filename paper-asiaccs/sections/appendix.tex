\subsection{Event record}
\label{sec:appendix:record}
Table~\ref{tab:record} is the \SI{592}{B} record every eBPF program fills
(\S\ref{sec:design:collector}). Offsets are bytes; every field is naturally
aligned, so the C and Go definitions carry no packing attributes and the
binary encoding of \S\ref{sec:design:wire} carries the record verbatim
after a \SI{16}{B} header (magic \texttt{0x31425357}, version, record
length, string length) and before the three resolved strings (user, group,
syscall name).

\subsection{Feature set v1}
\label{sec:appendix:features}
The 64-dimensional vector the MLP consumes is computed from a sequence of
syscall numbers (the whole trace when training and scoring in E1, the
per-process window of 100 when deployed) and is a line-by-line port of the
training script's \texttt{extract\_features}: six category frequencies
(I/O, file, memory, network, process, ``dangerous''); the relative
frequencies of the ten most common syscalls, ties broken by first
occurrence; the first ten syscall numbers scaled by $1/300$; the
frequencies of the ten most common bigrams; and ten statistics (scaled
length, distinct ratio, normalised entropy, peak-to-mean count, a dangerous
flag, repetition and ordering counts). Eighteen of the 64 slots are unused
and always zero; the category sets mix syscall numberings from different
ABIs, which we keep verbatim because both trained models depend on them.
Both facts are recorded in the module's source; the forest reaches its
accuracy on these features as they are.

\subsection{Baselines and training (E1)}
\label{sec:appendix:baselines}
All models see the same stratified folds. \textbf{\sys MLP}: 128-64-32-16
hidden units, trained in NumPy by mini-batch gradient descent with
balanced class weights and gradient clipping at learning rate 0.01, batch
32, up to 200 epochs with early stopping on AUPRC over a
\SI{10}{\percent} validation split of the training fold (patience 25);
weights loaded into the Wasm module for scoring. \textbf{STIDE}: 6-gram
database over the training fold's normal traces; score is the fraction of
novel 6-grams in a trace. \textbf{Isolation Forest}: scikit-learn, 300
trees, fit on the training fold's normal traces' v1 features; score is the
negated anomaly score. \textbf{Random Forest}: scikit-learn, 300 trees,
balanced class weights, on v1 features. \textbf{LSTM}: embedding of size
32, one LSTM layer of 64 units, sequences truncated to 1,000 syscalls,
Adam at $10^{-3}$, batch 128, up to 40 epochs with patience 5 on the same
validation split (36,097 parameters). \textbf{\sys RF}: the Random Forest
of the same (seed, fold), serialised by \texttt{forest\_export.py} into the
module's image (depth-first nodes of 12 bytes: split feature, float32
threshold or leaf probability, right-child index; the left child is the
next node) and scored through \texttt{rf\_score}. Each float64 threshold
$t$ is stored as the largest float32 $\le t$; for every float32 $x$,
$x \le t$ iff $x \le \mathrm{floor}_{32}(t)$, and the rounding the exporter
implements equals that floor for finite $|t| < 3\cdot10^{38}$. Both
statements were discharged by Z3 as floating-point SMT problems (the files
are in the repository), so the only difference from scikit-learn is the
float32 storage of leaf probabilities, averaged in float64. Operating
points: the 95th percentile of the training fold's normal scores (nominal
\SI{5}{\percent} FPR) and the F1-optimal threshold on the training fold.
Seeds control fold assignment and model initialisation; the three seeds
re-shuffle the same 5,951 traces, so the 15 runs are not independent and
the confidence intervals in Table~\ref{tab:e1} are optimistic.
